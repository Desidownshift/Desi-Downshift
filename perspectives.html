A Sanctuary for the Living

The sun was dipping below the ridge, painting the sky in bruises of purple and gold, as we trudged down the footpath to our house. My legs were aching, my mood wasn't light and the acids in my stomach had started to munch on my muscles as we walked past the town’s old cemetery.

In the gathering dusk, the wrought-iron gates looked like black teeth, and the tombstones cast long, distorted shadows across the grass. A cliche chill ran down my spine. Wanting to shake off the unease with a bit of bravado, I nudged him with my elbow, nodding toward the graves. "So," I said, a haughty, teasing smirk on my face, "you ever have the guts to walk through there at twelve midnight?"

I expected a laugh, or maybe a defensive boast. Instead, he looked over at the quiet rows of stone. "Why would I be scared at midnight?" he asked quietly. "Uh, because it's a cemetery?" I scoffed, crossing my arms. "It’s literally where dead people are. It's supposed to be haunted." He smiled, a gentle, grounded sort of smile, and shook his head. "You've got it all wrong. A cemetery isn't a place for the dead. They don't care about the stones or the grass anymore. It’s a place for the alive."

I blinked, my haughty attitude suddenly deflating. "What do you mean?" "Think about it," he said, gesturing toward a headstone, "We build these places because we’re the ones who need them. It's a sanctuary we visit to keep their memories around us, to remind ourselves once in a while of who they were and what they taught us. Every stone is just a love letter left behind by someone who is still missing them."

"Every Sunday, people bring flowers, a couple of beers, candles, light them, have a happy time with their family reminiscing the people who added values to their life in the past." He started walking again, his voice dropping to a warm, comforting cadence that seemed to wrap around the cool evening air. "There’s nothing to fear in a place built entirely out of love and remembrance," he continued. "It’s not a house of horrors. It’s just a quiet garden where the living come to say 'I still remember you.' It’s actually pretty beautiful."

I stood there for a second, looking back at the cemetery. The sharp, terrifying angles of the tombstones suddenly seemed to soften. The shadows didn't look like lurking monsters anymore; they looked like a blanket tucked around centuries of stories.

By the time I caught up to his stride, the fear was entirely gone, replaced by a strange, blooming warmth. I had looked at those gates my whole life and seen an end, but my friend had just shown me that they were actually a monument to the things that never die.
