The Platform Philosophy

I was sitting at the railway station the other day, just watching the yellow glow of the overhead indicator board flicker. I was late, and naturally, I felt that familiar, sharp prick of annoyance in my chest. I had a schedule to keep, a life to meticulously organize. But as I looked down at my ticket, then out at the dark red paan spit tracks, my mind drifted.

We are all building a puzzle, aren't we? Except, no one gave us the box with the picture on it. Every morning, we wake up to scattered, mismatched pieces, and we just... fit them together, blindly, trusting that by the time the sun goes down, a new fragment of the picture will make sense.

People love to ask, "What’s next? What’s the plan?" As if life is a map we hold in our hand. Sitting there on that cold bench, I just smiled at the sky, because honestly? I don't have a perfect reply. I don’t know if tomorrow the heavy rain will fall, or if the train I need to catch will even come at all. I don’t know if my plate will hold a feast when moon ascends, or where this winding, broken road eventually ends. But if the final chapter was already clear and sage, why would we read till the last page?

I looked up at the sky. A few drops were starting to hit the tin roof above me.

x + y = z
Life is just this giant, quiet equation. On that platform, I realized I didn't know what x was. I had no clue about y. And z was a total mystery. But that is the most breathtaking part of being alive, watching time slowly fill in the blanks.

Sometimes, x is just a detour on a road you thought you knew, a sudden, silent heartbreak that tears your world in two. And sometimes, y is just a stranger, walking softly through the crowd, who turns into the quiet voice that makes your world less loud. And only at the very end, when you look back from whatever destination you reach, you realize that all along, z was exactly where you were supposed to land.

A loud whistle broke the silence, and the headlights of the train finally cut through the darkness, reflecting off the wet iron tracks. Every single step is just beautiful, utter randomness. We panic on these platforms, thinking we are lost, thinking a delay means a failure. But life has this funny way of circling back.

As I stood up and adjusted my bag, a sudden truth took flight:
The shadows of the unknown aren't here to steal the light.
Uncertainty is not a ghost that's waiting by the track,
It’s not a storm that's trying hard to hold your spirit back.
It is the spark, the open door, the wind upon the sea—
The only thing that makes the ride the way it's meant to be.
